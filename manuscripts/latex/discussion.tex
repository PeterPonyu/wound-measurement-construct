\section{Discussion}
The central finding concerns the interpretation of a tissue-level expression readout. Across the discovery specimens, the mean fibroblast-associated loading closely followed the fraction of cells above a fixed cut. Collapsing repeated specimens to patients preserved the association, and patient resampling supported its stability. A higher mean therefore carries substantial information about cell-state abundance. It does not by itself establish a stronger programme within each cell. The exact mean decomposition separates the abundance and within-state terms, but their empirical correlation does not identify a causal contribution or prove constant state-specific intensity.

This distinction complements expression-programme decomposition and compositional analysis \citep{kotliar2019,buettner2021}. An expression programme can be reproducible while its biopsy-level average changes with the sampled cellular mixture. Gene labels describe the fitted loading pattern; they do not establish a new cell type or an exclusive mechanism of repair. Independent measurements of state-specific expression and tissue abundance would be needed to resolve the remaining biological ambiguity.

The technical controls make several simple explanations less plausible. State labels survived count matching, and immune-transcript exclusion preserved specimen summaries. Dissociation and ambient contamination are established concerns in single-cell analysis \citep{vandenbrink2017,young2020}, so these checks support interpreting the pattern as more than one obvious processing artifact. The placebo residual analysis also gives a useful limit: multiple tissue-composition covariates reduce residual bimodality. The current data cannot uniquely separate the loading from every compositional influence.

Clinical interpretation remains more restricted. The strongest high-state fraction occurred in healthy skin, the healing comparison was imprecise after patient aggregation, and the apparent result depended on the threshold used to summarize cells. Patient omission preserved the direction of the small all-cell contrast, which argues against a single-patient sign reversal but does not establish prognostic performance. The contrast between detectable paired anatomy and uncertain healing discrimination indicates sensitivity to tissue differences without establishing healing specificity. These findings agree with the need to preserve biological replication and to separate exploratory associations from prediction-model validation \citep{squair2021,zimmerman2021,collins2015}.

The external projections extend tissue context rather than supplying a common validation endpoint. Cohorts differ in sampling, cell recovery and available labels; some lack patient mappings or healing outcomes, and the smallest projection has only two specimens. The inconsistent immune associations suggest that tissue context deserves direct measurement in a future study. Pooling these cohorts as if they measured the same patient-level prognosis would obscure their design differences.

Several limitations constrain the conclusions. The outcome cohort contains only 11 patients and the paired-anatomy control comes from the same discovery source. The frozen encoder was trained on the expression cohort subsequently used for the internal discrimination audit. State definitions are data-derived, and threshold and topic-capacity sensitivity remain relevant. The source outcome is a 12-week category, not an individual healing time. Power and equivalence calculations quantify sensitivity under stated assumptions; a nonsignificant test or a data-derived margin cannot establish a clinical null \citep{lakens2017}. These limitations motivate a prospective study with an independently specified patient outcome, a clinically justified effect margin, standardized tissue sampling and a frozen analysis. Such a design would test whether a measurement with substantial abundance semantics adds useful prognostic information.

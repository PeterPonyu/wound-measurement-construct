\papertitle{Mixture weight and programme strength: construct validation of single-cell topic loadings in diabetic foot skin}
\section*{Abstract}
\noindent A higher mean single-cell programme score can reflect either stronger expression within cells or more cells occupying a high state. We examined this distinction before interpreting a fibroblast-associated topic as a diabetic-foot-ulcer (DFU) healing marker.
A frozen 15-topic model represented 76,461 cells from 25 selected foot-skin samples. We assessed distributional semantics, technical artifacts, analysis-constant sensitivity, negative controls and external projections. An author-derived mapping collapsed 14 DFU samples to 11 patients; anatomical controls and four representation benchmarks used the corresponding analysis units.
Across 25 samples, mean fibroblast loading correlated with the threshold-defined high-state fraction at Spearman correlation 0.933 (sample-bootstrap 95\% interval 0.789--0.983). The highest fraction, 0.841, occurred in healthy non-diabetic skin. Depth matching preserved 99.5\% of state labels, and exclusion of cells carrying immune transcripts preserved sample weights (Spearman correlation 0.989). Semantic associations persisted over tested thresholds and topic numbers; the healthy-skin counterexample failed at 10 topics, and healing-arm significance was threshold-sensitive. The corrected all-cell mean-loading contrast in 11 patients was 0.0280 (permutation p=0.2719; bootstrap interval [−0.0017, 0.0631]). Patient-level differences for the within-fibroblast mean, high-state fraction and fibroblast composition were 0.0892, 0.1514 and 0.0708, respectively; all three bootstrap intervals included zero and their BH-adjusted q values were 0.470. The separate 9-versus-5 high-state-fraction design had 24.9\% simulated power at its observed effect. Patient-level topic, module, PCA and NMF AUCs were 0.714, 0.786, 0.500 and 0.125; these exploratory estimates do not establish clinical superiority. Paired anatomy remained detectable in 10 patients. External cohorts did not supply independent patient-level healing validation.
The mean loading carries information about the prevalence of high-scoring cells, while state-specific means also vary. Its correlation with the threshold fraction does not prove invariant within-state intensity, and the tested fibroblast pattern remains an unvalidated prognostic marker. Patient-level validation requires independently defined outcomes and a clinically justified effect margin.
\par\smallskip\noindent Keywords: topic model; cell-state abundance; construct validity; DFU; CHI3L1; TIMP1; patient-level inference.

\section{Introduction}
Diabetic foot ulcers develop in tissue where neuropathy, vascular impairment, inflammation and repeated injury can coexist. Closure does not remove the risk of recurrence, making it important to understand which tissue measurements describe the current wound and which might inform its subsequent course \citep{armstrong2017,wilkinson2020}. Single-cell profiling has identified fibroblast-associated programmes that differ between healing groups in diabetic-foot skin \citep{theocharidis2022,geo_gse165816}. These observations motivate prognostic research, but a programme score first needs a defensible measurement interpretation. An average expression loading can change for several reasons, and those reasons imply different biological experiments and different validation requirements.

Fibroblasts are particularly relevant to this problem because their transcriptional diversity reflects both tissue organization and responses to injury. Experimental lineage studies in mouse skin distinguish fibroblast populations with different contributions to dermal architecture and repair \citep{driskell2013}. Single-cell comparisons across organs identify substantial variation in extracellular-matrix programmes and distinguish fibroblasts from related mural populations \citep{muhl2020}. A broader atlas describes shared and specialized fibroblast states across tissues and disease contexts \citep{buechler2021}. These findings support careful study of fibroblast heterogeneity; they do not imply that a gene programme observed in one wound cohort corresponds to a universal cell type or carries the same clinical meaning in another.

The tissue context also matters within a nominally common cell class. Human skin profiling has associated aging with changes in fibroblast transcriptional organization \citep{soleboldo2020}. Anatomical site, the depth and location of a biopsy, and the cells recovered during processing can therefore be relevant to the interpretation of a tissue summary. A score enriched for matrix and inflammatory genes may capture a reproducible aspect of that context without being specific to successful repair. The central question is whether differences in its mean primarily describe how strongly cells express the programme, how many high-scoring cells are recovered, or a combination of these contributions.

Topic models and gene-expression decompositions provide ways to represent such heterogeneity without requiring every cell to belong to one expression programme \citep{blei2003,kotliar2019}. Neural latent-variable models offer another means of summarizing high-dimensional expression for subsequent comparisons \citep{lopez2018}. Their fitted coordinates nevertheless acquire meaning through the loss function, gene panel, normalization and sampling scheme. A simplex constraint makes the coordinates compositional; it does not independently calibrate a coordinate as the probability of a biological state \citep{aitchison1980}. Changes in cell composition and changes within a compartment must consequently be distinguished when interpreting a biopsy-level average \citep{buettner2021}.

Defining a high state by a threshold makes one aspect of the measurement easy to communicate, but it also discards variation within the two resulting bins. The general information loss from dichotomizing continuous measurements is well established \citep{altman2006}. In single-cell analysis, neighborhood-based abundance methods provide an alternative to treating every cluster boundary as biologically fixed \citep{dann2022}. Neither approach removes the need to specify the denominator and independent sampling unit. A correlation between a mean and a threshold fraction is especially difficult to interpret mechanistically because both summaries are calculated from the same cell scores. An explicit decomposition of the mean can expose the terms that a simple abundance interpretation treats as approximately constant.

Technical effects can modify both the score and the cells assigned to a compartment. Tissue dissociation can induce expression programmes, extracellular RNA can enter measured profiles, and doublets can combine transcripts from different cell types \citep{vandenbrink2017,young2020,wolock2019}. Quality control, depth matching and exclusion rules address particular parts of this problem, with consequences that depend on the retained population \citep{heumos2023}. Across studies, a common representation also has to balance preservation of biological variation against technical alignment \citep{luecken2022}. Technical robustness therefore supplies evidence about the stability of a measurement under specified perturbations, while leaving its disease specificity and prognostic value to be evaluated separately.

Clinical inference imposes a further distinction between cells, specimens and patients. Repeated biopsies can characterize tissue heterogeneity, but they do not create independent healing outcomes. Methods for multi-sample expression and abundance comparisons preserve this dependence rather than treating every recovered cell as a replicate \citep{crowell2020,squair2021,zimmerman2021,lazic2010}. Outcome discrimination is only one aspect of a clinically useful predictor: agreement between predicted and observed risks and the consequences of decisions at relevant thresholds also matter \citep{steyerberg2010,vancalster2019,vickers2006}. Reporting and validation should match that intended use \citep{collins2015}. In a small discovery cohort, selecting among readouts can produce optimistic performance estimates, and a nonsignificant contrast does not establish equivalence \citep{cawley2010,schuirmann1987,lakens2017}. Requirements for prediction-model development depend on outcome frequency, model complexity and anticipated performance rather than a universal sample-count rule \citep{riley2019}.

The biological material should remain visible throughout this measurement argument. A dissociated-cell atlas describes the populations that survived sampling and processing, while gene-level summaries show the expression context from which a programme was inferred. Neither is a spatial reconstruction of the wound. Similarly, coloring an embedding by a score is a way to inspect its distribution, not an independent test of the score's biological validity. Embedding algorithms can alter apparent separation and density, making their connection to the input measurements and the inferential unit essential to interpretation \citep{becht2019,huang2022visualization}.

We examine a frozen fibroblast-associated topic in public diabetic-foot skin data as a tissue measurement and an exploratory healing readout. The analysis distinguishes mean loading within fibroblasts, the fraction above a fitted threshold and the mean over all recovered cells. Distributional decomposition and technical perturbations examine what these readouts measure. Author-mapped patient comparisons and paired anatomical controls examine which contrasts they can support, while external wound datasets extend the tissue contexts in which the frozen score can be evaluated. This design connects measurement interpretation to clinical inference without assuming that evidence for one is sufficient for the other.
